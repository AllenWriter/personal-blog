## 2. Feng Shui Basics

### 2.1 What is feng shui?

The lecture broke feng shui down into three understandable layers:

#### (1) Environmental science

Do you like living in a place with plenty of sunlight, clean and bright? Of course you do. That's how the environment affects people: a place bathed in sunlight naturally has better feng shui.

> "Feng shui is actually very simple. Would you like to live in a place that is very dirty and dark? Definitely not. So a place where sunlight floods in and the view is pleasant has better feng shui a bit. That is environmental science." Segment 1 00:07:26

#### (2) Energy-field science (magnetic field matching)

Just as some plants like water and others like dryness, a person's birth year, month, day and hour are like a "seed" that needs to find the right "soil" and "climate."

> "Some plants suit dry conditions, and some need a lot of water. Soil cultivation and hydroponics are different. If you get it backwards, the plant will rot. The year, month, day and hour you were born are the seed; you need to find the right magnetic field for it." Segment 1 00:08:10

#### (3) Psychology

The height and width of a space affect people's moods. For example, a 2.1-meter ceiling height makes people feel oppressed, while a tall lobby makes them feel comfortable.

> "Look at that three-way overpass outside the window: it looks uncomfortable and unpleasant, and it affects your mood. This lobby has very high ceilings, so don't you feel comfortable? If the ceiling were only 2.1 meters, would you be happy? No. So when you buy a home, buy one with a higher ceiling. That's psychology." Segment 1 00:09:18

### 2.2 Why do Chinese houses "face south" (sit north, face south)?

This is a big-data conclusion built up over a thousand years of experience:

1. **Best natural light**: In the Northern Hemisphere, a house that sits north and faces south gets the most sunshine throughout the year
2. **Shelter from the wind and cold**: In winter, the northwest wind blows, and a tall building behind the house can block the cold wind
3. **Ancient needs**: Without electric lights in ancient times, people relied on sunlight, so facing south gave the best light

> "China is in the Northern Hemisphere, and a house that sits north and faces south sees the most sunlight on average. More than 1,000 years ago there were no electric lights, so people relied on sunlight coming in. Another reason is that in winter the northwest wind blows, and a high wall at the back blocks it, so when you buy a home, try to choose one facing south." Segment 1 00:12:28

**Special case**: In Chongqing, where it is cloudy year-round, local people don't care much about orientation when buying a home, because there is little sunlight to begin with.

## 5. Choosing a Home and Feng Shui

### 5.1 Common misconceptions about choosing a floor

**Common online misconception**: believing a certain number (such as the 8th or 18th floor) has especially good feng shui, or believing low floors are "grounded" while high floors lack "earth energy."

**Correct understanding**:

1. **The floor number is psychology**, and each region has different habits (in Hong Kong people like the 8th, 18th and 28th floors, while in Shanghai people avoid the 18th and 4th floors)
2. **Heaven, earth and humanity**: Ancient feng shui said "earth energy" because buildings only had three floors. Modern high-rises have 30 to 40 floors, so there is not just "earth energy" but also "sky energy."

### 5.2 The right way to choose a floor

**Core principle**: Look at the external environment, not the number

#### Step 1: Look at the external view

- If the 6th floor faces a sharp corner outside or an overpass points at the window → not good, switch to the 8th floor
- If the 8th floor faces a mountain grave outside → not good, choose another floor

#### Step 2: Consider natural light

- **Older communities** (narrow spacing between buildings) → choose a middle to high floor
- **Newer communities with wide spacing** → low floors are fine

**Advantages of middle to high floors**: more sunlight and more yang energy. Middle to low floors with close spacing between buildings get poor light and heavy yin energy.

### 5.3 Floor layouts to avoid

- ❌ **Door facing door** (drafty "through-hall sha"): the front door faces the balcony door or a window, creating a "through-hall sha" that lets wealth energy flow straight out
- ❌ **Missing corners**: an irregular floor plan with recesses affects the family's fortune
- ❌ **Poor door orientation**: see "Doorway direction and carpet color" above

### 5.4 Surroundings to avoid

❌ **Surroundings you should not buy into**:

- Too close to a hospital (lots of bacteria and viruses)
- Right next to an elevated bridge or expressway (heavy sha energy, noisy, and it affects mood and decisions)
- Facing a chimney
