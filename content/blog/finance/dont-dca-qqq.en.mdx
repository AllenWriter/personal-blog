## Core Logic: SMA200 Trend Filter


The core logic we test is very simple: use the SMA200 with a buffer band to filter out bear market cycles. During bull markets, we use reasonable leverage to amplify returns.

First, a quick explanation of the SMA200. It is the 200-day moving average, which institutions widely recognize as the dividing line between bull and bear markets. But we made one key improvement: instead of using the SMA200 directly as a buy or sell point, we add a buffer band.

### Specific rules are as follows:

- **Buy rule**: we only enter when the price is more than 4% above the SMA200
- **Sell rule**: we only exit when the price is more than 3% below the SMA200

### Why go to all this trouble?

Let's use a stock called XYZ as an example. Suppose XYZ's SMA200 is $100. Without a buffer band, if the price drops to 99 you sell everything, and if it rises to 101 you buy back in. If the market keeps whipsawing around 100, you get slapped in the face again and again, and your capital is quickly ground away by commissions and slippage.

With a buffer band, however, the buy price becomes 104 and the sell price becomes 97. We only buy when the trend is strong enough to break above 104, and we only sell when the trend has truly broken down below 97. This 7% range is our airbag, designed to filter out meaningless noise.

The logic behind this is simple: **catch the big moves, let go of the small ones**. We would rather miss a big bounce off the bottom than trust a fake trend. We would rather give back some profit than run too early and only leave when a real bear market arrives.

One more note: in all the tests below, whether QQQ, QLD or TQQQ, we use the 200-day moving average of QQQ as the reference.

## The Ultimate Solution: Core-Satellite Asset Allocation


So are we at a dead end? QQQ is too slow, the QQQ moving average filter is too timid, the TQQQ moving average filter is too dangerous, and the QLD moving average filter is good but not extreme enough.

The question is: is there a way to combine the stability of QQQ, the high returns of QLD and the explosive power of TQQQ, while keeping risk genuinely within an acceptable range?

**The answer is yes. This is the ultimate solution I'm giving you today: core-satellite asset allocation.**

### The specific recipe

| Position | Asset | Strategy | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60% (core) | QQQ or VOO | Buy and hold forever | Ballast. It makes sure you ride the tech dividend and never miss a rally, earning the market's average return (β) |
| 30% (satellite) | QLD (2x leverage) | SMA200 moving average filter | Growth engine. It uses QLD's sweet-spot effect to amplify returns while risk stays under control, earning the excess return (Alpha) |
| 10% (gamble) | TQQQ (3x leverage) | SMA200 moving average filter | The lottery ticket. Treat it like buying a call option: if it goes to zero, you only lose 10% of total assets, but if it multiplies 90 times, you can leap into a new class. It earns convexity return (Convexity) |

### Execution logic

- **Buy signal**: when QQQ's price is above SMA200 × 1.04, the buying window for the satellite and gamble positions officially opens
- **Sell signal**: when QQQ's price falls below SMA200 × 0.97, this 40% of the money must follow discipline strictly and be fully sold into cash
- **Core position**: the 60% core position never moves, no matter what happens

**Practical note**: To keep the data rigorous, the backtest goes all in the moment a signal appears. In real life you don't need to be so rigid. Once the signal is confirmed, you can choose to dollar-cost in over several periods, or wait for a small pullback and add on dips. Building the position in batches smooths your cost basis and makes the psychological pressure smaller. The key is to finish building the position within the trend-confirmation window, not to agonize over which day's price you got.

## Why not keep a cash position?

### First: the strategy has a built-in dynamic cash pool

Many friends ask: shouldn't I keep some cash to buy the dip? What if it keeps falling?

My answer is this: the 40% satellite portion of this strategy is essentially your dynamic cash. Remember, the core of the SMA200 moving average filter is: **fully invested in bull markets, all cash in bear markets.**

When the market truly crashes into a bear market, your QLD and TQQQ will all be sold, and your account will automatically hold 40% in a big pile of cash. That 40% is your safe harbor in the bear market, and also the most powerful ammunition you have when the next bull market starts.

So you don't need to keep extra cash. The system automatically keeps you in cash when you should hold cash, and keeps you invested when you should be invested.

### Second: avoid cash drag

If you still hold 20% cash in a bull market, waiting for an opportunity, quantitative investing calls this "cash drag." TQQQ's annualized return in bull markets can reach 60% or more. Every dollar you leave sitting idle wastes the dozens-fold compounding that dollar could have delivered.

Since we have already used the SMA200 to control downside risk, when the signal says it's safe, we must push forward at full speed and be fully invested. That is what makes the risk we are taking worthwhile.

### Third: logical consistency

We do trend following, not left-side bottom fishing. Holding cash is usually meant for buying more as prices fall. But our strategy is trend following, which is right-side trading. Our logic is: when the price breaks the moving average, the trend has gone bad, so we run rather than buy.

If you still hold cash and use it to average down after the price has broken the moving average, you violate the original purpose of the stop-loss and turn into catching a falling knife. So in this system, cash is there to save your life, not to add to positions.

### Fourth: separate investment money from emergency savings

I must give a risk warning here. When I say don't keep cash, I mean the money in this investment account. As for your emergency savings for life (six months of living expenses), please keep them honestly in a bank time deposit or a money market fund. That money must never go into the stock market. **Never use your food money to buy TQQQ. That is gambling with your life, not investing.**
